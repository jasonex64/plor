# TV game-show presentation and "host" characters as an identity source for Plor / Offbeat

Researcher's note on reliability. About half of the planned page fetches failed (Medium 403, fandom 402, breezewiki blocked, one 404, two redirects not followed), so several findings rest on search-result summaries of a page, not on a full read. Those are marked *(snippet only)*. Anything I know from memory but could not source in this round is kept out of "Cited Findings" and listed under "Gaps" as *unverified background*. No GDC talk, Fonts In Use writeup or Codrops article was actually read; where one was found by URL only, it is listed as a pointer.

## 1. Shows closest to Offbeat's mechanic: what makes each reveal and graphics package work, and who made them

### Takeaway
The reveal devices that carry these shows are single, physical-feeling objects that do one thing slowly: Pointless's column of 100 blocks counting down, Family Feud's flip-disc board snapping an answer into place, Wheel of Fortune's letter cells turning. Design credits are thin in public sources: the firms that are documented are build and playout companies (Kinetic Pixel, XL Video, Ferranti-Packard) and one branding studio (Rough Collie for Only Connect), and none of them published a design-intent writeup I could read.

### Cited Findings

**Pointless (BBC)**
- The scoring column is an LED wall showing a stack of 100 graphic blocks; it counts down from 100 and stops at the answer's score once a contestant has answered *(snippet only)* — [Ventuz: "Ventuz Powers UK Game Show Pointless"](https://ventuz.com/?p=2229) (the URL now serves a generic Ventuz product page; the article text was not retrievable), [Panorama Audiovisual, 2015](https://www.panoramaaudiovisual.com/en/2015/02/26/el-grafismo-3d-en-tiempo-real-de-ventuz-realza-la-popularidad-de-pointless-en-bbc/) (404 on fetch).
- In-studio graphics are run by Kinetic Pixel (a Caerphilly company) on the real-time graphics software Ventuz; they also drive the contestant desk screens showing each team's score and the HD projection behind the hosts *(snippet only)* — [Caerphilly Observer on Kinetic Pixel](https://caerphilly.observer/news/1000079/kinetic-pixel-caerphilly-company-tv-biggest-shows/), [Kinetic Pixel site](https://kpx.tv/?p=10096).
- The tower hardware was built by XL Video: a three-section custom metal column fabricated by Shapes (Belgium) carrying a Barco NX6 LED display, assembled on set in about two and a half hours *(snippet only)* — [LEDs Magazine](https://www.ledsmagazine.com/specialty-ssl/automotive-vehicles/article/16677821/xl-video-creates-a-smart-led-display-for-pointless-game-show-scoring-tower).
- The set was refreshed in 2017 after eight years on air; no designer is credited in the coverage found — [HuffPost UK](https://huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/pointless-set-new-makeover_uk_59db71bee4b046f5ad997c01).

**Family Feud / Family Fortunes**
- The original 1976 board was a magnetic flip-dot display built by Ferranti-Packard of Ontario, on a system patented in 1964; each dot was physically flipped by electromagnets and the units were built by hand — [The Strong National Museum of Play, "Reviving the Family Feud sign"](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).
- Its size: 24 modules in a 6 by 4 array, 35 flip-discs per character, eight lines of 30 characters (240 characters), 8,400 discs in all — [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).
- Staff typed answers on a QWERTY keyboard and the board revealed them on demand, with a kinetic flourish; the high-contrast discs read clearly on TV cameras. The board was on every episode from 1976 to 1995 and was used on foreign versions in as many as 80 countries — [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).
- The board was restored in December 2023 after a donation by Randy West and is planned for public display from 2027 — [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).
- A fan wiki instead describes the first 20 years as a "trilon" board; this conflicts with the museum's account, which I rate as the more reliable *(snippet only)* — [Game Shows Wiki](https://gameshows.fandom.com/wiki/File:2.png); contradicted by [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).
- The Face-Off buzzer sound is reported to be a recording of a telephone ringing that Mark Goodson chose *(snippet only, attributed by the search summary to the Strong's archive blog)* — [The Strong, National Archives of Game Show History blog](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/category/national-archives-of-game-show-history/).
- In the UK Family Fortunes, a wrong answer puts an X on the board with a buzzer that differs from the US one *(snippet only, fan source)* — [All 80s blog](https://80sblog.all80s.co.uk/family-fortunes/).

**Wheel of Fortune**
- Until 1997 the puzzle board was a manually operated grid of trilons in four rows (11, 13, 13, 11; some counts add four corner units for 52). The last trilon puzzle aired on 21 February 1997 and a board of 52 touch-activated monitors debuted three days later; letters no longer had to be loaded by hand between rounds *(snippet only)* — [Wikipedia: Trilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilon), [TV Insider](https://tvinsider.com/1033201/wheel-of-fortune-big-moments-history).
- The show has a dedicated "Used Letter Board" (an off-camera display of letters already called) with its own history page *(URL found, page not read)* — [Wheel of Fortune History Wiki: Used Letter Board](https://wheeloffortunehistory.fandom.com/wiki/Used_Letter_Board).
- One source dates the touch-screen switch to 2013; this contradicts the 1997 date given by several others — [extensionerp page](https://erp.extensionerp.com/celebtrends016/wheel-of-fortune-2013-the-year-the-iconic-game-show-went-digital-and-struck-gold.html); contradicted by [Wikipedia: Trilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilon).

**Only Connect (BBC)**
- Rough Collie was commissioned by the producer, Presentable, to brand the quiz and produce its titles and on-screen graphics. The studio's page shows screenshots but gives no account of the design reasoning — [Rough Collie](https://roughcollie.tv/?p=1057).
- Rough Collie also designed the web version of the Connecting Wall: it made a template, then a prototype to confirm the game played well and stayed faithful to the show; Innivo built the mechanics into the BBC framework — [Rough Collie](https://roughcollie.tv/?p=1057).
- The clue-picker symbols were Greek letters at first and were replaced by Egyptian hieroglyphs (two reeds, lion, twisted flax, horned viper, water, eye of Horus); sources disagree on whether the change came in series 4 or 5 *(snippet only)* — [Wikipedia: Only Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_Connect).

**Countdown (Channel 4)**
- The clock is the central piece of the set and has changed many times; a fan wiki keeps its history *(URL found, page not read)* — [Countdown Wiki: Countdown clock](https://wiki.apterous.org/Countdown_clock). Stage One has a project page for a Countdown set build *(not read)* — [Stage One](https://stageone.co.uk/project/countdown).

### Inferences
- Pointless is the nearest model for Offbeat's rarity bonus. Its column turns "how obscure was that?" into a single falling quantity that the audience watches stop. Offbeat's inverse of Family Feud could show an answer's commonness the same way: one bar or count that runs and stops, low being good.
- Family Feud's board worked because it was a real object with a fixed character grid, visible latency and a noise. On a phone, that translates to a reveal that is typed into fixed cells, not faded in, and that has a short, non-zero delay.
- Wheel of Fortune's used-letter display is the direct precedent for Offbeat's taken letters: a persistent alphabet strip where a taken letter visibly goes dark, kept in the same place all game.
- Only Connect shows that a calm quiz can be branded with one small set of odd symbols instead of lights and noise. That fits the owner's complaint that the earlier pixel scenes and 3D village "went too far".
- Public credit for these packages goes mostly to hardware and playout vendors. A report writer should not claim a named designer for Pointless or Family Feud.

### Gaps
- No design-intent writeup was found for any of these shows (why the Pointless column counts at the speed it does, why the Feud X looks the way it does). The Kinetic Pixel and Ventuz case-study pages exist but could not be read.
- Nothing was found in this round for: Richard Osman's House of Games (graphics studio, design), Blockbusters, The Chase, or the Jeopardy board. The one search that covered House of Games returned nothing relevant.
- No Fonts In Use entry for any TV show here was found or read.
- *Unverified background (from memory, not sourced this round):* Pointless's column fills from 100 downwards with a rising tone and a distinct sound for a "pointless" (zero) answer; Family Feud's strike is a large red X with a harsh buzzer and three strikes pass control; the Countdown clock runs 30 seconds with a well-known music cue; Only Connect's wall is a 4 by 4 grid whose solved groups change colour and move to the top; The Chase uses a stepped board with the chaser closing a gap. These are widely known but need a source before the report states them.

## 2. Game-show set and graphics aesthetics translated to screens, and games that use the framing

### Takeaway
The best-documented translation of a game show to a screen is You Don't Know Jack and its descendants, which drop the set altogether and let a voice, pacing and type do the work; its makers wrote the method down as The Jack Principles. I found no reliable writeup on bulb marquees, LED scoreboards or podiums rendered in web UI, so that part rests on inference.

### Cited Findings
- The Jack Principles are design guidelines for an "interactive conversation interface", written by Harry Gottlieb of Jellyvision as a 1997 white paper and presented at the Game Developers Conference that year. They came out of making the You Don't Know Jack host sound as if he were really there *(snippet only; the Medium article returned 403 and the wiki pages were blocked)* — [The Charming Device: "Please Read The Jack Principles"](https://medium.com/the-charming-device/please-read-the-jack-principles-4a7ee4820875), [Jackbox wiki mirror: iCi](https://breezewiki.discard.no/jackboxgames/wiki/ICi).
- The pacing rules include giving the user one task at a time and limiting the number of choices at any moment; the "illusion of awareness" rules include responding with human-seeming intelligence and emotion to the user's actions, inactions and past actions *(snippet only)* — [The Charming Device](https://medium.com/the-charming-device/please-read-the-jack-principles-4a7ee4820875).
- Jellyvision applied the same principles outside games, in a scripted HR product (ALEX), with no natural-language processing or machine learning *(snippet only)* — [The Charming Device](https://medium.com/the-charming-device/please-read-the-jack-principles-4a7ee4820875), [ClickZ profile of Harry Gottlieb](https://www.clickz.com/digital-mad-man-you-dont-know-jack-founder-harry-gottlieb/44666/).
- For the 2011 You Don't Know Jack, the team's stated decision tests were "what's simpler?" and "what's funnier?", and they kept the core format because its appeal was its simplicity — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/q-a-jellyvision-insists-i-you-don-t-know-jack-i-again).
- You Don't Know Jack is framed as a fictional trivia game show for one to four players, played for virtual money *(snippet only)* — [Wikipedia: You Don't Know Jack (2011 video game)](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Don%27t_Know_Jack_(2011_video_game)).
- Fonts In Use has an entry on the typography of You Don't Know Jack Volume 3 and a Jellyvision tag *(URLs found, pages not read)* — [Fonts In Use: YDKJ Volume 3](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/73925/you-don-t-know-jack-volume-3), [Fonts In Use: Jellyvision tag](https://fontsinuse.com/tags/47728/jellyvision).
- Trivia Murder Party set out to make players feel they were inside a clichéd horror film, with recognisable tropes to reference and subvert; its host is voiced by Spencer Ham, a late swap from Andy Poland (VP of Audio and Editorial), who said the change was probably best for pacing *(snippet only)* — [Jackbox blog: Trivia Murder Party 3 dev diary #3](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/trivia-murder-party-3-dev-diary-3), [Jackbox blog: dev diary #5](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/trivia-murder-party-3-dev-diary-5).

### Inferences
- The Jack Principles support the first research round's finding: the show feeling comes from timing and a voice that seems to notice the player, not from drawing a studio. For Offbeat this argues for spending the motion budget on two or three reveal moments (the host's answers appearing, the rarity result, a letter being taken) and leaving the shell plain.
- "One task at a time, few choices" already matches Offbeat's loop (one question, one text field, 20 seconds). The missing part is the reaction to inaction and to past actions: a line when the clock runs low with nothing typed, a line that recalls yesterday's result or the current streak.
- Trivia Murder Party shows a host can carry a whole genre framing through voice and copy alone. A light theme carried only by the host's wording would cost far less than the pixel-art or 3D attempts.
- Set dressing (bulbs, marquee, podium) is the part of the game-show look most likely to repeat the "too busy" problem. A single object in the Pointless or Feud manner is the safer borrow.

### Gaps
- The original Jack Principles document was not read in this round. The full list of principles (pacing, illusion of awareness, maintaining the illusion) should be taken from the first research round or from the primary PDF, not from this note.
- No source was found on You Don't Know Jack's kinetic type or question-intro animation; the one search returned nothing relevant.
- Nothing was found or searched successfully on: Fall Guys' show framing, Telltale or Netflix game-show games, game-show-styled web games, or CSS treatments of bulb marquees and LED scoreboards. No GDC talk on Jackbox host writing was found; the search stated none appeared.
- *Unverified background:* You Don't Know Jack never shows its host; each question gets an animated number sting and the screen is mostly moving type on a dark ground. Fall Guys frames its rounds as a televised show with "episode" and "qualified / eliminated" language.

## 3. Host characters in light games: where the personality comes from, minimal ways to show a host, and writing reactive lines for daily play

### Takeaway
In the documented cases the personality is written first and everything else follows: Jellyvision's team writes from a shared idea of who Cookie is, and Duolingo spent months on backstory and voice rules before voice and animation. The documented risk is the one Offbeat faces on daily play: a sharp-tongued host wears on people, and Duolingo had to soften Lily's sarcasm so it did not discourage users. Advice on avoiding repetition is consistent across game-writing sources (many variants, cooldowns by category, per-line memory, trigger only on a real change) but comes from weaker sources.

### Cited Findings

**Writing versus voice versus drawing**
- Cookie's personality was built from the writing, which was in turn shaped by Tom Gottlieb's performance; new writers are taught to "get inside Cookie's head", and that shared sense of the character is described as the fastest way to learn the game's style — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/q-a-jellyvision-insists-i-you-don-t-know-jack-i-again).
- The team was a small core of writers plus contract writers from Chicago's improv and stand-up scene and some writers from earlier Jack games, with structured training, weekly feedback and writers editing each other; an editor watches for jokes repeating across the game — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/q-a-jellyvision-insists-i-you-don-t-know-jack-i-again).
- Jellyvision auditions writers: candidates write samples, then are asked to write Jack-style questions *(snippet only)* — [Kotaku on head writer Steve Heinrich](https://kotaku.com/you-dont-know-jack-lead-writer-says-being-funny-is-seri-5922913).
- The team treats Cookie as the star of the game and keeps continuity on him; a writer's joke about Cookie's father was caught as a continuity error *(snippet only)* — [CGMagazine interview](https://www.cgmagonline.com/?p=2087).
- Duolingo's team spent months on each character's personality, backstory and relationships; an established character lets a short line carry motivation without explanation. Each character then got a custom text-to-speech voice cast by audition — [Duolingo blog: character voices](https://blog.duolingo.com/character-voices).
- Duolingo openly asked how deadpan Lily can be without hurting the learning experience, and noted that sarcasm does not carry the same way across languages — [Duolingo blog: character voices](https://blog.duolingo.com/character-voices).
- Reporting on the Lily chatbot says her sarcasm was toned down because, too sarcastic, she could demotivate learners; she was kept playful but encouraging *(snippet only, secondary source)* — [AIM Research](https://aimresearch.co/?p=12445).
- Fast Company reports Lily as an emo teen whose voice direction was to say positive lines with monotone disdain *(snippet only)* — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90664438/duolingo-wants-to-be-the-sesame-street-for-adults).
- Duolingo's writing guide describes Duo as helpful, persistent and slightly awkward, says he speaks through text or speech bubbles, and says he must always stay in character; the brand voice is brief, direct and active, with tone changing by situation while the voice stays the same *(snippet only; the guide URL now redirects to the blog's design hub)* — [Duolingo design: Duo](https://design.duolingo.com/writing/duo), [Duolingo design: voice](https://design.duolingo.com/writing/voice).

**Showing a host with little art**
- Duolingo does not hand-animate speech: with more than 40 languages and ten characters, mouth movement is generated *(snippet only)* — [Duolingo blog: world character visemes](https://blog.duolingo.com/world-character-visemes).
- Secondary writeups describe the pipeline as speech to phonemes to visemes (a small set of mouth shapes), with timings sent to Rive, which blends the shapes at runtime; Rive's own blog says Duolingo used Rive for phonetic lip sync *(snippet only; the DEV posts are by a third-party agency)* — [Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/creative-technologists-duolingo-s-solution-to-the-designer-to-developer-handoff), [DEV Community](https://dev.to/uianimation/how-duolingo-uses-rive-for-their-character-animation-and-how-you-can-build-a-similar-rive-mascot-5d19).

**Reacting to the player, including to doing nothing**
- The Stanley Parable narrates inaction: analysis notes that even refusing to play is often scripted and commented on, so not choosing is treated as a choice — [Fistful of Wits analysis](https://fistfulofwits.com/2014/02/15/game-analysis-the-stanley-parable/).
- Its narrator remembers earlier play: in the broom closet he reacts differently on repeat visits, and on a later run resolves not to react because that would only encourage the player *(snippet only)* — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/time-extend-the-stanley-parable).
- A GDC narrative review notes players come to such a voice already distrusting it because of Portal's GLaDOS, an all-seeing voice that turns on the player *(snippet only)* — [GDC Vault narrative review, 2019](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/GameNarrativeReview/dicovskiy_chris_paper.pdf).

**Not getting annoying on repeat play**
- Write far more variants of each line than needed and keep the best few — [Game Developer: "8 key principles of writing effective game dialogue"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/8-key-principles-of-writing-effective-game-dialogue) *(snippet only)*.
- Set cooldowns per category of line so important lines get through and filler backs off; fire a line only when the game state has really changed; track recently used lines so one strong line cannot recur too often even after a cooldown ends *(snippet only; the source has formatting faults and no clear author, so treat as weak)* — [PulseGeek: bark timing and cooldown](https://pulsegeek.com/articles/dialogue-barks-timing-and-cooldown-best-practices/).
- Some repetition is useful for recognition; variation should cover wording and the gap between repeats, not only the pool size *(snippet only)* — [Sound in Games: Requisite Variety](https://soundingames.dei.uc.pt/index.php/Requisite_Variety).

### Inferences
- The order of leverage for Offbeat's host is writing, then timing, then a small mark, then voice. Both documented teams fixed who the character is before anything was drawn or recorded. A one-page character sheet for the host (what it wants, how it talks, what it never says) is the first deliverable, ahead of any art.
- Offbeat's premise gives the host a ready-made motive that most mascots lack: it holds the secret answers and takes letters, so it is the player's opponent. Lily's case is the warning about how far to push that. A host who is smug when the player matches and grudging when the player finds a rare answer fits the mechanic; one who mocks the player is the version Duolingo backed away from.
- Minimal embodiments that the evidence supports: a speech bubble (Duo's stated channel), and a mouth with a handful of shapes driven by the line being shown (the viseme idea, cut down). A face made of type or a single glyph that changes with state would be the cheapest form, with no pipeline at all.
- The four Offbeat events each map to a Jack-Principles category: a near-miss and a rare answer are reactions to an action; an empty field near the end of the clock is a reaction to inaction; a streak or "you said that last week" is a reaction to past actions; "most people said..." is a comparison.
- For daily play, the bark advice translates to: a pool per event, never the same line two days running, some lines that fire only rarely so there is still something unseen after weeks, and silence as a valid outcome. The Stanley Parable's "resolves not to react" is a useful precedent for the host sometimes saying nothing. Any counts or rates would have to come from the owner or a ticket; none of the sources gives a number worth adopting.
- Voice audio is the most expensive and most intrusive channel on a phone used in public. The sources do not cover mute settings, but text-first with optional sound follows from Duo "speaking" in bubbles.

### Gaps
- No source was found on mute or frequency settings for a host, or on how often daily players tolerate host lines. This is the most important unanswered question for Offbeat and needs either owner judgement or testing.
- Nothing was found on Wordle-likes with a mascot, Alphabear's bears, or Untitled Goose Game's goose; these were not searched individually for lack of budget.
- No Valve writing postmortem on GLaDOS was found; the GLaDOS material here is second-hand comparison only.
- No official Duolingo writing guide for Lily was found; only Duo's exists in the results, and its page now redirects, so its text was not read in full.
- Jackbox's own "Making of" and "Behind the scenes" blog posts ([Party Pack 6](https://www.jackboxgames.com/making-the-jackbox-party-pack-6), [Party Pack 10 directing](https://www-origin2.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-directing)) were found but not read; they are the most likely place for first-hand notes on host writing.
- *Unverified background:* Alphabear rewards a round with a generated sentence built from the player's own words, read by a bear, which is a cheap "host noticed what I typed" device; Untitled Goose Game gets its character almost wholly from animation and one honk, with no text.

## 4. Split-flap, flip-board and scoreboard animation on the web

### Takeaway
There are many small split-flap components for React, Svelte and plain web components, most using two half-height flaps per character rotated in CSS 3D; a non-3D variant that slides glyphs vertically gets a similar read for less work. I found no measured phone performance data, only practitioner claims, so any choice needs a test on a real phone.

### Cited Findings
- React options include `react-split-flap-effect`, and `@daformat/react-split-flap-display`, described as zero-dependency with a pure-CSS 3D flip *(snippet only)* — [npm: react-split-flap-effect](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-split-flap-effect), [pkgstats: @daformat/react-split-flap-display](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:@daformat/react-split-flap-display).
- A framework-free custom element, `@hot-page/hotfx-split-flap`, animates a string in the manner of a station board *(snippet only)* — [jsDelivr: hotfx-split-flap](https://www.jsdelivr.com/package/npm/@hot-page/hotfx-split-flap).
- `svelte-split-flap` has no external dependencies and includes sound *(snippet only)* — [GitHub: svelte-split-flap](https://github.com/socketopp/svelte-split-flap).
- `split-flap-ascii` flips each cell through intermediate characters before settling on its target *(snippet only)* — [pkgstats: split-flap-ascii](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:split-flap-ascii).
- Older references: the jQuery plugin Flapper, and a 2012 HTML/CSS3/JS split-flap by Jaume Sanchez whose post warns the code may be outdated — [npm: jquery-flapper](https://www.npmjs.com/package/jquery-flapper), [clicktorelease](https://www.clicktorelease.com/blog/split-flap-display.html).
- The common CSS technique is two halves per character: the top flap rotates from 0 to -90 degrees, then the bottom from 90 to 0, with the old glyph showing until the midpoint; one gist advances letters on each `animationiteration` event *(snippet only)* — [chatium: 3D CSS flip-down clock](https://chatium.com/blog~3d-css-animation-flip-down-clock), [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/doc22940/8c6d760daea12ed4d875cf70d7192ba9).
- A cheaper variant avoids 3D and slides digits vertically with a transition *(snippet only)* — [freefrontend: sliding digital clock](https://freefrontend.com/code/animated-sliding-digital-clock-2026-02-27/).
- Practitioner advice: update only the cells that changed; under reduced motion, set the flaps to `animation: none` so the cell snaps to the new glyph; fewer flips save battery on phones. A claim of smooth 60 fps on older hardware for pure-CSS flips comes from an informal developer post, not a benchmark *(snippet only)* — [DEV: minimal flip clock](https://dev.to/jeason_li_2a921781568e802/building-a-minimal-flip-clock-in-the-browser-astro-react-lh6), [Fern UI: Splitflap](https://fern-ui.vercel.app/explore/splitflap), [FlipCloc performance guide](https://flipcloc-about.pages.dev/how-to-use/performance).
- The physical original for comparison: Family Feud's board was 8,400 magnetic flip-discs, 35 per character — [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/reviving-the-family-feud-sign/).

### Inferences
- Offbeat's surfaces are small (a handful of answer rows, a 26-letter strip, a score), far below the cell counts where flip animation would strain a phone. That is my reasoning, not a measured result.
- A flip-disc (dot matrix) reveal is closer to the Feud original than a split-flap is, and is simpler to build: dots that turn from dark to light in a short stagger need only opacity or a 2D transform, with no 3D context.
- The project already has a reduced-motion setting (per the owner's memory notes), and the snap-to-glyph pattern above is the standard way to honour it.
- Each of these packages is small and little maintained. Given the repo's "build for today" and surgical-diff rules, writing the single effect needed in CSS is likely a better fit than adding a dependency; that is a design decision for the ticket, not a research finding.

### Gaps
- No Codrops article on split-flap or scoreboard effects was found; the search for one returned community examples only.
- No measurements of 3D flip cost on phones were found. Download counts, last-release dates and bundle sizes were not checked for any package.
- Nothing was found on LED-scoreboard, seven-segment or bulb-marquee effects in CSS; these were not searched for lack of budget.
- Sound for flips (the clack is much of the effect on TV) was not researched, beyond noting that one Svelte package ships it.
