# Designers and case studies behind the visual identities of admired light games

Researched 2026-11-16. "Opened" means I read the page itself. "Search summary" means I only saw a search engine's summary of the page and did not open it; treat those as leads, not facts.

## Puzzmo: who designed the brand and game UIs, and is there a case study?

### Takeaway
I found no published case study, Fonts In Use entry, talk or credit for Puzzmo's visual design, and no named art director or agency. The public material on Puzzmo is about game design and engineering, not its look.

### Cited Findings
- (Opened) Game Developer's interview with Zach Gage names only Gage (co-creator, designs the games) and Orta Therox (lead engineer), with Hearst as owner; it says nothing on typography, palette, motion or who designs the visuals. Its design principles are about play: every game is a "sandbox", and an idea is interesting when it connects to what a player already knows. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/puzzmo-co-creator-zach-gage-on-building-newspaper-games-that-can-last-forever)
- (Opened) The Six Colors interview with Gage on Pile-Up Poker contains nothing on visual design, UI polish or animation; the only theming remark is that money was chosen because it "has a really good flavor" and relates to poker. — [Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2024/08/interview-game-developer-zach-gage-on-pile-up-poker-and-resisting-dark-patterns/)
- (Search summary) Game Developer's Pile-Up Poker pieces say the team iterated toward more legible card art and added outlines to show where cards can be placed, and name Saman Bemel-Benrud (games programmer) and Jack Schlesinger (game designer). — [Game Developer, prototyping](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/here-s-how-zach-gage-prototyped-puzzmo-sensation-pile-up-poker); [Game Developer, cancellation](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-pile-up-poker-survived-cancellation-to-become-a-puzzmo-hit)

### Inferences
- With no agency or art director credited anywhere I looked, the look is probably in-house and led by Gage, whose earlier games share the flat, typographic style. This is my inference, not a sourced fact.
- The one visual lesson on record is functional: legibility first, outlines to mark where an action is allowed.

### Gaps
- blog.puzzmo.com could not be opened by either fetch tool or the browser pane (blocked), so Puzzmo's own posts, including anything on the 2025 redesign, are unread. Someone with a normal browser should check it; this is the most likely home of primary material.
- Four searches for Puzzmo's typefaces, palette, motion rules, brand designer and 2025 redesign returned nothing relevant. No Fonts In Use entry surfaced.
- Podcasts with Gage (Hey Good Game, Real Time Strategy) appeared in results but I did not listen to or read them.

## NYT Games: redesign, per-game identity, typefaces, icon system

### Takeaway
The best primary source is designer Caro Turcios's portfolio case study of the 2022 NYT Games marketing brand refresh, which sets out a per-game system: simplified gameboards, "gems", game icons, one palette per game. The Creative Review piece is paywalled after its intro, and I found no published credits for the 2024 app redesign or for typefaces.

### Cited Findings
- (Opened, in a browser) The 2022 "NYT Games Brand Refresh" was done in-house: creative director Ida Woldemichael, art direction and design Caro Turcios. — [Caro Turcios portfolio](https://www.carolinaturcios.com/nyt-games-brand-refresh)
- (Opened) The problem: the previous marketing brand was "visually beautiful but often too abstract" to show what the games are, especially to new audiences. The concept: put gameplay at the heart of the brand so the games introduce themselves, balanced with whimsy and the Times's refined design language. — [Caro Turcios portfolio](https://www.carolinaturcios.com/nyt-games-brand-refresh)
- (Opened) The system's components, tailored per game: game icons (brand markers, used sparingly); "gems" (small delightful fragments of a game); simplified gameboards (real boards reduced to essentials); XS simplified gameboards (icon-like, for small sizes); and true-to-UI gameboards (mirror real play, fully editable, for walkthroughs). — [Caro Turcios portfolio](https://www.carolinaturcios.com/nyt-games-brand-refresh)
- (Opened) Its seven rules for individual game brands: follow the master voice; simplify game UI; a module system for flexibility; animate gameplay when possible; colors unique to each game yet cohesive across the brand; use game logos as design elements; keep a subtle nod to the previous brand. — [Caro Turcios portfolio](https://www.carolinaturcios.com/nyt-games-brand-refresh)
- (Opened) The brand is split in two levels: the overarching Games brand (shows breadth, "something for everyone") and individual game brands (gameplay, distinct palettes, details). Spelling Bee has a character, Beeatrice, with animation loops and secondary art. Games covered: Crossword, Mini, Wordle, Sudoku, Spelling Bee, Letter Boxed, Tiles, Vertex. — [Caro Turcios portfolio](https://www.carolinaturcios.com/nyt-games-brand-refresh)
- (Opened) Jonathan Knight, head of games, calls the app redesign rolled out at the start of 2024 the end of a multi-year rebrand from "Crossword" to "Games"; it won a 2024 Apple Design Award. The Friends and Me tabs were overhauled in 2025. No designers are named. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/from-crossword-to-community-inside-nyt-games-two-year-transformation/)
- (Opened, intro only; rest paywalled) Creative Review's 7 July 2026 article by Adam Woodward interviews Knight on a "consistent, human-crafted approach"; the readable part gives only play statistics (11.2 billion plays last year, 4.2 billion of them Wordle). — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/new-york-times-games-wordle-crossplay-design-jonathan-knight/)
- (Search summary) TechCrunch reported the 2024 redesign introduced new game cards and cut the tab bar from five tabs to three after testing. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2675705)
- (Search summary) Heidi Erwin's LinkedIn says she led game design on Strands (March 2024) and built Pips's puzzle creation system. This is game design, not visual design. — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/heidierwin/)

### Inferences
- The transferable structure is two-level: one shared parent (voice, type, layout) and, per game, a palette, a simplified board that works as the game's picture, and a small set of "gem" fragments. For Plor, each game's own board state (a letter tile, a substring chip) can be its logo and its marketing image.
- "Animate gameplay when possible" suggests motion identity should come from the game's own moves, not from decoration added around it.
- The case study has no typeface names or hex values; the page is mostly images, which my text read does not capture.

### Gaps
- Typefaces (Karnak, Franklin, Cheltenham variants) were not confirmed by any source I opened. One search summary noted "NYT Karnak Condensed" on a scraped comparison site of uncertain reliability; I did not open it.
- No credits found for the 2024 app redesign, the in-app icon system, or Pips and Strands visual design. Lian Chang's involvement is unverified; nothing I opened names her.
- No It's Nice That or Brand New coverage of NYT Games surfaced. A portfolio link for a Lauren Gatta NYT page returned 404.
- The Creative Review body text remains unread (paywall).

## Jackbox: the named artists and per-game style material

### Takeaway
Jackbox's own Party Pack 10 art interview is the primary source: each game gets an art lead who picks a distinct reference world, while structure and general UI are shared. I found no public style guides or character sheets on ArtStation or Behance.

### Cited Findings
- (Opened) Bruno Rodríguez (lead artist, Fixytext): theme of malfunctioning machines; tried pure ASCII art, was unhappy, settled on a hybrid of hand-drawn and rendered objects with a technical-drawing feel; weighed visual hierarchy so art would not compete with writing and music. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) Kyle Fleischer (lead artist, Dodo Re Mi): ties visuals to mechanics so players understand what they are doing; a music game needed a reason for players to make sounds, hence birds on a wire; references were botanical illustration and 1960s jazz album covers; feedback that the game lacked tension produced a plant that eats poor performers; the sky is never shown, to differ from Tee K.O. 2. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) Stef Egan (lead artist, TimeJinx): a busy but welcoming lab in a UPA cartoon style; blocky simple shapes so backgrounds do not distract from characters. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) Hero Paul See (game artist, Tee K.O. 2): honor the original and push effects further; stresses knowing when to cut scope; daily check-ins and weekly art meetings. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) Owen Watson (art lead and art director, Hypnotorious): references are Billy & Mandy, Rocky & Bullwinkle, Ren & Stimpy; the main constraint was time, since art was designed while the game design was still moving. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) The pack's menu ("picker") was designed by Lev Cantoral and Charlie Bickett. Rodríguez's view: the games share structure and general UI but differ in art, which creates contrast and memorability. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)
- (Opened) The article does not discuss typography or color for any game. — [Jackbox blog](https://www.jackboxgames.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-of-pp10-art)

### Inferences
- Jackbox's method per game: one owner, one real-world reference family (jazz covers, UPA cartoons, technical drawings), a look that explains the mechanic, and a deliberate check that no two games in the set share a setting or mood.
- The shared shell plus distinct art is the same two-level structure as NYT Games.

### Gaps
- I did not find or open personal portfolios for the named artists; one search for ArtStation and Behance material returned only job listings. Dave Innis was not in the article I opened.
- No per-game style guide, type choices or palettes are published.

## Duolingo, Alphabear, Monument Valley, Mini Metro, Balatro

### Takeaway
Duolingo and Monument Valley have the richest first-hand material, and both say the same thing: fix a small set of constraints (silhouette and shape language; camera and palette) and judge everything against them. Mini Metro and Balatro add color-plus-shape accessibility and a single free pixel font used everywhere.

### Cited Findings

**Duolingo**
- (Opened) "Reshaping Duo" (Greg Hartman, 20 November 2013) describes the owl redesign for a single consistent look across platforms. Color and species were fixed; the silhouette was the priority, since details cannot rescue an unappealing shape. Months of iterations were shared with the team daily, and a slight quarter-turn was chosen as his best angle. No agency is named; the post speaks as the in-house team. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/reshaping-duo/)
- (Opened) The Vikram redesign (Derek Gieraltowski and Samia Khalaf, 4 April 2024): shapes carry personality "for better or for worse". Circles read as soft and friendly but also jolly or lazy; his torso went from a sphere to a pill with straight sides and rounded ends, with a sensitivity consultant and an employee committee checking each step. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/vikram-redesign/)
- (Opened) Lip-sync (Jasmine Vahidsafa and Kevin Lenzo, 10 November 2022): ten characters each have 20+ mouth shapes, drawn per character to fit its personality; a Rive state machine runs mouth states alongside body states, plus idle behaviors (nods, blinks) and correct/incorrect reactions, exported as a single runtime file. A design guide was written before animating. Rive was chosen over a game engine for compact files. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/world-character-visemes)
- (Opened) Rive's piece on Duolingo names Jeff Masters (first creative technologist), Alex Chopjian (associate creative director of animation), Clint Fanelli and Hazel Jennings. The Rive file is the production asset, replacing mockups plus written specs that engineers rebuilt in code; a creative technologist owns state machine logic and documents each input; assets are templated for reuse. — [Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/creative-technologists-duolingo-s-solution-to-the-designer-to-developer-handoff)

**Monument Valley (ustwo games)**
- (Opened) Art director Ken Wong: the team accepted discarding "70, 80, 90 percent" of its work. The camera is fixed to an isometric view, which also narrows the palette (isometric art is typically about three colors). "We removed a lot of tools from the artist's toolbox"; nothing is there just for decoration. The starting reference was an M.C. Escher drawing; ideas borrowed from Braid and Fez were cut. — [Kill Screen](https://killscreen.com/monument-valley-elegance)
- (Search summary) Other accounts say each screen was designed to stand as a work of art, and concept images were pinned to the wall before anyone knew if they worked as levels. — [Treehouse](https://blog.teamtreehouse.com/designer-profile-ken-wong-monument-valley); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/stairway-to-heaven-the-making-of-monument-valley)

**Mini Metro / Mini Motorways (Dinosaur Polo Club)**
- (Opened) Jamie Churchman's GDC 2017 talk on Mini Metro's visual design starts from "understanding your subject matter": ask what the game is about, break it into parts, design UI around them. The page gives no further specifics; the talk is on YouTube. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-behind-the-minimalistic-visual-design-of-i-mini-metro-i-); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kmHXk4Y35QM)
- (Opened) The studio designed both games with a color-blind mode from the start; Mini Metro pairs colors with shapes on a neutral background. Mini Motorways drew on novelty tourist maps with bright colors. Disasterpeace's procedural score is driven by game data. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/-i-mini-motorways-i-and-the-delicate-art-of-marrying-complexity-and-minimalism)

**Balatro (LocalThunk)**
- (Opened) Fonts In Use identifies the game's font as m6x11, a freeware pixel font by Daniel Linssen, used throughout and often animated with a bounce; contributed by "fangly", 8 February 2025. — [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/65816/balatro-computer-game)
- (Search summary) Secondary write-ups say the art was drawn with a mouse in Aseprite under fixed palette and resolution rules, and that the CRT look is GLSL shaders on the LÖVE canvas. I opened none of these and found no LocalThunk interview on the font or shaders. — [blakecrosley.com guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro); [foro3d](https://foro3d.com/en/2026/mayo/el-arte-del-crt-digital-balatro-y-la-magia-de-love.html)

**Alphabear (Spry Fox)**
- (Search summary) The only lead is an interview naming Noemi Gomez as Spry Fox's lead artist, in a piece about Cozy Grove, not Alphabear. — [The Avocado](https://the-avocado.org/2021/11/05/indie-developer-interview-spry-fox/)

### Inferences
- A small team can copy the method without the budget: name three or four things that never change per game (a shape family, a palette, one typeface, one camera or layout) and treat removing options as the design work.
- Duolingo's shape language is a cheap lever for giving two games different characters: a game built from pills and circles feels different from one built from blocks, before any color is chosen.
- Balatro shows one free typeface plus one signature motion (the bounce) and one screen treatment can carry an entire identity.
- Duolingo's Rive pipeline is sized for a large team; the portable part is "the animation file is the shipped asset" and a short written list of states (idle, correct, wrong) before animating.

### Gaps
- Duolingo's brand guidelines site (design.duolingo.com) now redirects to the blog's design hub, so its illustration and shape rules could not be read. The 2019-era refresh's authorship (Johnson Banks or in-house) is unconfirmed; a search summary pointed to a Design Week article I did not open ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/2-6-january-2019/duolingo-owl-receives-makeover-as-app-becomes-more-gamified)).
- No Alphabear art direction source found at all.
- Churchman's talk content was not watched; only the article summary was read.
- Monument Valley typefaces and exact palette rules were not found.
