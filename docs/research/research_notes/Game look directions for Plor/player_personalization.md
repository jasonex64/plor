# Player-facing personalization and customization in light, social, daily games (and how it shows to friends)

Researched 2026-11-16. Counts marked "repo count" were taken that day from the products' public source repositories. Anything in a Gaps list marked *unverified (memory)* was not checked against a source in this session and must not be treated as fact.

## 1. Theme and skin systems: how other products define and offer them

### Takeaway
The products with very large theme catalogues (Monkeytype, Lichess) get there by making a theme a tiny, fixed set of data (about ten colour tokens, or one image) rather than hand-styled screens, and by taking contributions from a community. Products that sell or gate looks (Chess.com, Discord, Tetris 99, Nintendo) keep the catalogue small or make each look an event reward, and show the choice to other people only through a small mark next to the name.

### Cited Findings
- Monkeytype defines every built-in theme as one object of ten hex colour tokens (`bg`, `main`, `caret`, `sub`, `subAlt`, `text`, `error`, `errorExtra`, `colorfulError`, `colorfulErrorExtra`) plus an optional `hasCss` flag, validated by a schema. — [monkeytype `frontend/src/ts/constants/themes.ts`](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/blob/master/frontend/src/ts/constants/themes.ts)
- Repo count on 2026-11-16: that file holds 187 theme entries; 52 of them set `hasCss: true` and have an extra hand-written stylesheet in `frontend/static/themes` (52 `.css` files). So about 72% of Monkeytype's themes are pure data, and about 28% carry per-theme CSS that someone has to maintain. — [themes.ts](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/blob/master/frontend/src/ts/constants/themes.ts); [static/themes folder](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/tree/master/frontend/static/themes)
- Third-party documentation pages for Monkeytype disagree on the count (56, "400+"); neither matches the repo count, so they should not be quoted. — [Mintlify themes guide](https://www.mintlify.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/guides/themes); [Mintlify features page](https://www.mintlify.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/features)
- Monkeytype also lets players build a custom theme in settings from the same colour tokens; richer themes with custom CSS arrive by pull request from the community. — [Monkeytype GitHub discussion 688](https://www.github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/discussions/688)
- Lichess (repo count on 2026-11-16): 43 entries in the piece-set folder, 61 files in the board-image folder (files, not necessarily 61 distinct boards), and sound sets named standard, piano, nes, sfx, futuristic, robot, woodland, instrument, lisp, other. — [lila `public/piece`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/tree/master/public/piece); [lila `public/images/board`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/tree/master/public/images/board); [lila `public/sound`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/tree/master/public/sound)
- An older Lichess blog post reported 16 boards and 9 piece sets, so the catalogue has grown several-fold over the years; a user blog puts built-in piece sets at about 40, consistent with the repo count. — [Lichess blog, June improvements](https://lichess.org/blog/VZFSxCsAAMYDYv8Y/improvements-to-lichess---june-edition); [Lichess user blog on LiChess Tools](https://lichess.org/@/TotalNoob69/blog/customizing-lichess-pieces-boards-sounds-and-more-with-lichess-tools/1nQoshDg)
- Lichess board, piece and sound choices are private to the player's own screen; the only personalization other players see is "flair", added in 2023: an optional small graphic next to the username, described by Lichess as "an extra bit of personalisation and colour". — [Lichess Year in Review 2023](https://lichess.org/blog/ZZQVPhAAACEAw2ZR/-)
- Lichess flair is a pick from a fixed emoji-style list of 3,644 entries in eight categories (repo count on 2026-11-16: people 2,325, objects 303, symbols 284, nature 213, smileys 145, travel-places 141, food-drink 129, activity 104). Viewers can switch flair display off in their display preferences. — [lila `public/flair/list.txt`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/blob/master/public/flair/list.txt); [Lichess forum reply on the flair setting](https://lichess.org/forum/redirect/post/D01lKZ2E)
- Chess.com bundles a "theme" as board + pieces + move sounds + background; its mobile help page cites 26 pre-made themes plus a custom one, and board or pieces can be swapped on their own. — [Chess.com support: change my theme](https://support.chess.com/article/1330-how-can-i-change-my-theme-android)
- Chess.com treats the choice as identity material in its own editorial ("What Your Board Theme Says About You", "What Your Chess Piece Style Says About You"). — [Chess.com article, board theme](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-your-chess-board-theme-says-about-you); [Chess.com article, piece style](https://www.chess.com/article/view/what-your-chess-piece-style-says-about-you)
- Chess.com's paid tiers add name flair: the membership page lists "Even more Custom flair icons" for Diamond. No source found listing Diamond-only boards or pieces. — [Chess.com support: premium plans](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8562418-what-does-each-level-of-membership-get-me)
- Tetris 99 themes are earned, not bought with money: a Maximus Cup event theme unlocks permanently at 100 event points (first place gives 100, second 50); from April 2021 (update 2.2.0) older event themes could be exchanged for tickets earned from daily missions, at 30 tickets each then and reported at two tickets each in August 2026. — [Wikipedia: Tetris 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_99); [GameSpot on the ticket exchange](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/tetris-99-update-lets-you-exchange-tickets-for-some-older-themes/1100-6490482/); [Nintendo Wire, 56th Maximus Cup](https://nintendowire.com/news/2026/08/04/tetris-99-celebrates-splatoon-raiders-with-new-theme-for-56th-maximus-cup/)
- Discord: beyond Light and Dark, more than 20 extra colour themes are a Nitro (paid) perk; its Shop, now open to all users, sells avatar decorations, profile effects, nameplates and profile frames, with Nitro giving member pricing and some exclusives. — [Discord blog: ways to customize](https://discord.com/blog/ways-to-customize-your-discord-experience); [Discord blog: Shop open to all](https://discord.com/blog/complete-your-look-in-the-shop-now-open-to-all); [Discord blog: avatar decorations](https://discord.com/blog/avatar-decorations-collect-and-keep-the-newest-styles)
- Duolingo's avatar creator (blog post dated 2023-07-19) lets a learner customise hairstyle, body shape, piercings, skin tone and accessories, claims "billions of possible combinations", lives on the profile page, and replaces the uploaded photo (once an avatar is chosen a photo can no longer be uploaded). — [Duolingo blog: avatar creator](https://blog.duolingo.com/avatar-creator/)
- Duolingo's October 2026 update describes limited-time events in which progress earns special suits for the avatar, so the avatar has become a place to hang earned, time-limited cosmetics. — [Duolingo blog: October 2026 update](https://blog.duolingo.com/october-2026-update/)
- Nintendo Switch Online "Missions & Rewards" (2022): members complete missions for 20 to 100 Platinum Points and spend them on icon elements to assemble a user icon: characters usually 10 points, backgrounds and frames 5. The icon is composed from three layers (character, background, frame). — [Nintendo: missions and rewards](https://nintendo.com/whatsnew/missions-and-rewards-added-to-nintendo-switch-online); [Nintendo Life guide](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/03/missions-and-rewards-added-to-nintendo-switch-online)

### Inferences
- Plor's five themes are small by Monkeytype's standard (187) but in line with products that hand-style each look. The decisive design variable is not the count but the definition: Monkeytype's "ten tokens, nothing else" is what makes 135 of its themes nearly free, and the 52 with their own CSS are where the upkeep lives. If Plor's games get distinct per-game looks, any theme that is more than tokens multiplies by the number of games.
- The two cheapest, most widely used ways a player's choice becomes visible to friends are (a) a single picked mark beside the name (Lichess flair, Chess.com flair) and (b) a layered icon built from a small parts list (Nintendo: character + background + frame). Both are a fixed list in code, need no image upload and no moderation, and render at one small size wherever a name appears.
- A player's colour theme is almost never shown to others in these products; it is a private comfort setting. "Personal" in the social sense comes from the mark by the name, not from the theme.
- Duolingo's path (photo upload, then built avatar, then event suits for the avatar) shows an avatar builder becomes a content treadmill: its value depends on new parts arriving.
- Plor's earlier ruling against player-built themes matches where the cost is: Monkeytype can afford custom themes because the whole interface reads from ten tokens.

### Gaps
- Words With Friends, Neopets, Habbo, Apple Game Center, Tetris Effect skins, and Wordle-like games with themes: not researched within this session's budget; no sourced findings.
- Chess.com's current counts of boards, piece styles and sound sets: the 26-theme figure is from a support page that may be dated.
- Whether Lichess published a rationale for choosing a fixed emoji list over uploaded images: not found. *Unverified (memory):* Lichess has no uploaded profile pictures, and flair fits that no-moderation stance.
- *Unverified (memory):* Apple Game Center lets players use a Memoji or initials as their avatar; Words With Friends has paid/earned tile styles and profile frames; Tetris Effect unlocks avatars and stage skins through level progress.
- No data found on what share of players ever change a theme in any of these products.

## 2. Earned cosmetics in daily games, and whether they bring players back

### Takeaway
The only first-party numbers found are Duolingo's, and they credit small, measurable lifts to celebrating a milestone (animation), not to owning a cosmetic. NYT Games added collectable badges in 2025; they are free, mostly private, and partly awarded retroactively. Evidence that earned cosmetics themselves drive return play in daily puzzle games was not found; the academic evidence on badges is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo (blog, 2022-01-31): showing the new streak-extension animation, including special milestone-day animations, raised the chance that a brand-new learner was still active seven days later by 1.7%. — [Duolingo blog: how the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Same post: letting learners hold two Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38% (relative); learners who reach a 7-day streak are 3.6 times more likely to finish their course (a correlation, not an experiment); over 6 million learners then had a streak of 7+ days. — [Duolingo blog: how the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Same post gives the reason milestones matter: going from day 2 to day 3 is a 50% increase, from day 200 to 201 is 0.5%, so long streaks need set-piece milestones to feel like progress. — [Duolingo blog: how the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Duolingo's milestone redesign (blog, 2022-01-21) set three goals: universal (the "on fire" metaphor did not translate everywhere, so they chose a phoenix), celebratory (treated like a game power-up), and shareable (a share card generated in-app). It reports only that early metrics showed more people keeping streaks and celebrating milestones, with no figure. — [Duolingo blog: streak milestone design](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation)
- Duolingo "Streak Society" rewards, per a Duolingo designer's case study: an exclusive phoenix app icon at 30 days, extra streak freezes at 100, and leaderboard and profile flair at 365. This is a secondary source for the exact thresholds. — [Dribbble: Duolingo Streak Society](https://www.dribbble.com/shots/19161257-Duolingo-Streak-Society)
- Analyst commentary describes the 30-day icon as a visible identity marker and says Duolingo reserves full custom celebrations for milestone days; the same commentary notes the app icon showing sadder versions of the mascot as a streak lapses, and the tension that streak freezes can be bought. These are third-party readings, not Duolingo statements. — [Deconstructor of Fun: Duolingo streaks](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/streaks); [Lazyweb research note](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/duolingo-retention-experiments.md); [Lenny's Podcast summary, Jackson Shuttleworth](https://rosetta.to/u/lennyspodcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- About 9 million Duolingo users were reported to hold a streak of a year or more (trade-press figure, not checked against Duolingo's filings). — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/subscription-models-live-off-habits-lessons-from-duolingos-retention-success/)
- NYT Games badges (help centre): available for Wordle, Connections, Strands and Spelling Bee. Types: repeatable feats with a counter (for example "Wordle in 2"), levelling badges (Spelling Bee Genius at 5, 10, 15, 25, 50), one-time streak badges (for example 7 days; existing players got one matching their highest lifetime streak), date-specific and holiday badges that can only be earned on the day, and special challenges. Some are awarded retroactively. — [NYT Help Center: Games badges](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/games-badges)
- NYT badges need no subscription (any account earns them; subscribers also progress through Archive play). They appear below the stats after a puzzle, as the "three most relevant" in the Stats panel, and in full under the Me tab. The help page does not say friends can see them. — [NYT Help Center: Games badges](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/games-badges)
- Third-party lists report Wordle's milestone badges count total puzzles played (for example 1,000 and 1,500) rather than consecutive days, and Connections streak badges at 7 and 14 days. — [Beebom: Wordle badges](https://beebom.com/puzzle/wordle-badges-list/); [Beebom: Connections badges](https://beebom.com/puzzle/connections-badges-list/)
- A study of 859 streak designs argues streaks work mainly through fear of losing them. — [Mobbin: Streaks are about losing](https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs)
- Academic caution: Hanus and Fox (Computers & Education, 2015; 71 students, four time points) found a gamified course with badges and a leaderboard produced lower motivation and lower exam scores than the plain course. The psychologist's own gloss is that the harm depends on conditions: the person starts out intrinsically motivated and the reward gradually becomes the reason; feedback that recognises effort can be informational and may help. — [Psychology of Games: overjustification and achievements](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2016/10/the-overjustification-effect-and-game-achievements/)
- Tetris 99 (see section 1) is the clearest "earned look" model in a light competitive game: a theme is the event prize, time-limited to earn, kept for good, with a catch-up route later. — [Wikipedia: Tetris 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_99)

### Inferences
- What is evidenced is that marking the moment works (1.7% on 7-day retention for new learners), at Duolingo's scale. No source found shows the unlocked object (icon, badge, theme) adding retention on top of the celebration. With a closed beta of friends Plor cannot measure effects of that size at all; the decision has to rest on taste and on what friends say.
- NYT's design choices are a useful low-risk template: count totals as well as streaks (a total cannot be lost, so it carries none of the loss-aversion pressure), award retroactively so day-one players are not behind, show three at most by default, and include "you had to be there" day badges, which are the cheapest kind of collectible for a daily game because the calendar supplies them.
- A streak-earned mark that friends can see (Duolingo's 365-day flair) is the point where an earned cosmetic and social visibility meet; it costs one icon and one rule.
- Plor players arrive already intrinsically motivated (they play with friends), which is exactly the condition under which Hanus's reading says added rewards can displace the original reason. That argues for recognition-style marks ("you did this") over reward ladders ("do this to get that").

### Gaps
- Puzzmo's achievements/"notables", profile and any cosmetics: Puzzmo's site and blog were not reachable by the search and fetch tools used, so nothing is sourced. The only sourced Puzzmo fact is that groups were renamed clubs in July 2024, that a first club needs no paid plan, and that clubs can define custom leaderboards with a score expression. — noted here because it could not be read at the primary URL; from a search summary of [Puzzmo blog, groups to clubs](https://blog-puzzmo-com-staging.vercel.app/posts/2024/07/24/groups-to-clubs). *Unverified (memory):* Puzzmo awards per-game "notables"/trophies on a profile and has paid-tier themes.
- Duolingo leagues and streak-flame variants as cosmetics: not sourced in this session beyond the Streak Society thresholds above. *Unverified (memory):* league tiers show as a coloured gem badge on the profile.
- Sudoku/crossword apps' unlockable themes or sound packs: not researched.
- No GDC talk, Game Developer article, Deconstructor of Fun or Mobile Free to Play piece specifically on cosmetics and retention in daily or word games was found and read in this session.
- A general search for peer-reviewed work on avatar customization and retention returned only spam mirror pages; no reliable study is cited here. *Unverified (memory):* there is published work linking avatar customization to identification and enjoyment (for example Turkay and Kinzer), which the report writer should not cite without checking.

## 3. Social visibility: what shows next to a friend's name, and share results

### Takeaway
What friends actually see of each other's personalization in these products is small and consistent: one mark next to the name, an avatar, and the share result. The share result is the most powerful of the three and works when it is spoiler-free, standard in shape, and personal only in its content.

### Cited Findings
- Lichess: flair is the one personal mark shown beside the username everywhere a name appears, and each viewer can turn all flairs off. — [Lichess Year in Review 2023](https://lichess.org/blog/ZZQVPhAAACEAw2ZR/-); [Lichess forum reply](https://lichess.org/forum/redirect/post/D01lKZ2E)
- Duolingo: at 365 days a learner gets leaderboard and profile flair, so the mark by the name is earned rather than picked. — [Dribbble: Duolingo Streak Society](https://www.dribbble.com/shots/19161257-Duolingo-Streak-Society)
- Duolingo rewards learners who share their Year in Review with a themed leaderboard badge reflecting their "learner style", tying a visible mark to a personal summary. — [Duolingo blog: Year in Review behind the scenes](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)
- Discord's visible-to-others cosmetics are layered on the identity card: avatar decoration (ring around the picture), nameplate, profile effect, profile frame. — [Discord blog: Shop open to all](https://discord.com/blog/complete-your-look-in-the-shop-now-open-to-all)
- Wordle's share grid was not invented by its author: players were already typing rows of coloured squares by hand, and Josh Wardle then built an automatic, spoiler-free share (his own tweet dates the button to 2021-12-16). Players grew from about 90 to over 300,000 in about two months. — [The Spinoff: those coloured boxes](https://thespinoff.co.nz/business/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault); [CUNY Pressbooks: The Wordle Effect](https://pressbooks.cuny.edu/inspire/part/the-wordle-effect-how-five-letters-changed-the-internet/)
- Duolingo's milestone share card is deliberately simple: mascot, fire imagery, one line of text, shareable without leaving the app. — [Duolingo blog: streak milestone design](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation)
- Puzzmo lets a club define its own leaderboard by writing a score expression over the day's games: here the group, not the individual, is what gets customised. — [Puzzmo blog, groups to clubs](https://blog-puzzmo-com-staging.vercel.app/posts/2024/07/24/groups-to-clubs)
- A smaller puzzle app (Everyday Puzzles) uses a chosen badge as the avatar and puts friends' new badges and levels in its feed. — [Everyday Puzzles help: profile, feed and friends](https://everyday-puzzles-en.helpscoutdocs.com/article/2621-about-social-profile-feed-friends)

### Inferences
- Rules that keep it fun without clutter, drawn from the above: one mark per name, never several; a fixed size; chosen from a list (or earned), not uploaded; a viewer-side off switch (Lichess); and nothing that changes the layout of a leaderboard row.
- On a live board of friends' runs, the mark is read at a glance many times a day, so it does more "this is me" work than any profile page a friend rarely opens.
- Wordle's lesson for custom share-card styles: the grid spread because every share looked the same and was recognisable; the personal part was the pattern of the player's own attempt. Letting players restyle the share result would trade recognisability for expression. If the share card carries personalization, the safer place is the content (the player's own answer, their mark) rather than the frame.
- Group-level customization (a group's own name, mark or leaderboard rule, as in Puzzmo clubs) is a distinct lever from player-level customization and fits a friends-and-groups product.

### Gaps
- Reactions and emotes as personalization (custom reaction sets, signature emotes): no sources found or read.
- No research found on how many marks beside a name people can read before a leaderboard row feels cluttered.
- Whether NYT badges are visible to friends: the help page is silent.

## 4. "Personal" without customization: the game reflecting the player back

### Takeaway
The largest, best-evidenced "personal" feature in consumer apps is the recap built from the player's own data, and it needs no cosmetics at all. Spotify's and Duolingo's numbers show people both look at it and share it; NYT Games added one in 2025. Plor already stores the raw material (every Offbeat answer).

### Cited Findings
- Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached about 200 million engaged users in its first 24 hours, up 19% year on year (2024 took about 62 hours to reach that), and was shared more than 500 million times, up 41%; "engaged" means viewing at least one story, and shares include in-app shares, screenshots and downloads. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
- Duolingo Year in Review: shows total XP, minutes, lessons and more; Duolingo says it sees a lift in lessons completed and time spent after learners go through it, that a spike in new users followed the 2021 launch, and that millions share their cards. No figures are given. — [Duolingo blog: Year in Review behind the scenes](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes); [Android Authority: Year in Review 2025](https://www.androidauthority.com/duolingo-year-in-review-2025-3621782)
- Duolingo sets a floor for eligibility (at least 10 lessons in the year, per the 2023 release) so thin histories do not produce an embarrassing recap, and assigns each learner a "learner style". — [Duolingo investor relations news release](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-language-report-reveals-changes-top-10-languages)
- NYT Games launched "Year in Games" for 2025, a Wrapped-style personal recap of the player's daily puzzle play, in the iOS and Android apps and on a mobile web version. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/nyt-games-has-a-year-in-review-thing-now-too-203000878.html)
- NYT pairs the personal recap with whole-community facts: over 11.2 billion puzzles solved in 2025 (4.2 billion Wordle, 1.6 billion Connections), and the hardest Wordle, "Bongo" on 14 May, which ended 36,337 streaks. — [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/6436464/)
- NYT's retroactive badges are a smaller form of the same idea: the game shows it remembered what the player did before the feature existed. — [NYT Help Center: Games badges](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/games-badges)
- Lichess publishes a yearly community review rather than a personal one. — [Lichess Year in Review 2023](https://lichess.org/blog/ZZQVPhAAACEAw2ZR/-)

### Inferences
- A recap is the one personalization pattern here whose content cannot be copied by another player: the answers are the player's own. For Offbeat, which saves every answer, a personal history ("everything you ever answered", "your answer a year ago today", "the answer only you gave") is personal by construction and needs no art per theme or per game.
- The recap's share card is where "personal" and "visible to friends" meet at lowest cost: one card layout, the player's data in it.
- Recaps need not be yearly. NYT's badge counters and Duolingo's milestone days are the same reflection at smaller intervals (the 100th run, the first week). A beta that started on 2026-11-17 will not have a year of data for a long time, so the small-interval forms are the ones available.
- Pairing a personal fact with a group fact ("the day that broke the most streaks") suits a friends product: with a small group the equivalent is group-level ("the day everyone in the group gave the same answer").
- A minimum-activity floor (Duolingo's ten lessons) is worth copying so a recap never mocks a light player.

### Gaps
- No source found for a daily word game that exposes a browsable personal archive of the player's own past answers as a feature in its own right; the Offbeat idea above is an inference, not a documented pattern.
- No retention figures found for NYT's Year in Games or for Duolingo's Year in Review.
- Whether recaps help small-audience products or mainly serve as marketing at scale: no evidence found.

## 5. Costs and pitfalls: accessibility, maintenance, pay-to-look, clutter

### Takeaway
Cost scales with what a theme is allowed to touch. Token-only themes are cheap in any number; anything hand-styled multiplies by themes times games. The recurring pitfalls in the sources are paywalled looks, loss-driven streak pressure, and rewards displacing the reason people play.

### Cited Findings
- Maintenance evidence from Monkeytype: 52 of 187 themes need their own stylesheet on top of the ten tokens, so even a strict token model accumulates per-theme special cases. — [themes.ts](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/blob/master/frontend/src/ts/constants/themes.ts); [static/themes folder](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/tree/master/frontend/static/themes)
- Monkeytype's schema names error and "colourful error" colours as separate tokens from the main text colours, i.e. the states a typing game must keep legible are first-class parts of every theme. — [themes.ts](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/blob/master/frontend/src/ts/constants/themes.ts)
- Pay-to-look in practice: Discord puts 20+ themes and some decorations behind Nitro and sells the rest; Chess.com ties extra flair icons to its top tier; Nintendo's icon parts are subscriber-only. Duolingo's streak freezes can be bought with gems, which commentators flag as a tension between selling and the meaning of the streak. — [Discord blog: ways to customize](https://discord.com/blog/ways-to-customize-your-discord-experience); [Chess.com support: premium plans](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8562418-what-does-each-level-of-membership-get-me); [Nintendo: missions and rewards](https://nintendo.com/whatsnew/missions-and-rewards-added-to-nintendo-switch-online); [Lenny's Podcast summary](https://rosetta.to/u/lennyspodcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- The free counter-examples: NYT badges need no subscription; Lichess flair and all its boards, pieces and sounds are free; Tetris 99 themes are earned by play. — [NYT Help Center: Games badges](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/360011158491-New-York-Times-Games/games-badges); [Lichess Year in Review 2023](https://lichess.org/blog/ZZQVPhAAACEAw2ZR/-); [Wikipedia: Tetris 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_99)

### Inferences
- For one human plus coding agents, the cost ranking from cheapest to dearest is roughly: a pick-from-list mark by the name; counters and milestone marks computed from data already stored; a personal history or recap page; token-only extra themes; earned themes (each needs art direction and a contrast check in every game); an avatar builder (a parts catalogue that must keep growing). This ordering is an inference from how each system is defined in the sources, not a measured cost.
- Theme count times game count is the real multiplier for Plor if the two games get distinct identities: every theme must then be checked for contrast and for the timed-typing states (correct, wrong, time running out) in both games. Five themes times two games is ten surfaces today; each added theme or game adds a full row or column.
- A viewer-side "hide marks" switch and a "show at most N" rule are cheap insurance against clutter and are both in production at much larger products.
- In a closed beta among friends, paid looks have no audience and earned-only looks can read as status games between people who know each other; picked marks and personal history avoid both.

### Gaps
- No source was read on contrast failures in shipped theme systems, or on how Monkeytype or Lichess review contributed themes for contrast. *Unverified (memory):* WCAG 2.x asks 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and interface components; the report writer should confirm against the W3C text before quoting numbers.
- No per-theme maintenance hours or costs are published by any product surveyed.
- No evidence found on colour-blind handling of correct/wrong states across many themes in typing or word games. *Unverified (memory):* Wordle offers a high-contrast mode that swaps its two feedback colours.
